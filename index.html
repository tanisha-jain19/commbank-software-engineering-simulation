Tips for Financial Cybersecurity

Scammers, hackers, and identity thieves are constantly looking for ways to steal personal information and money. Following a few simple security practices can help protect your financial accounts and personal data.

Keep Your Software Updated

Keep your operating system, web browser, and security software up to date. Enable automatic updates whenever possible. Outdated software may contain security weaknesses that criminals can exploit to install malware or access your information. Be cautious of unexpected pop-ups or emails asking you to install security software.

Protect Your Personal Information

Treat personal and financial information like cash. Do not provide account numbers, passwords, credit card information, or other sensitive details unless you are confident that the request is legitimate. Be especially careful with unexpected emails, text messages, and phone calls asking for personal information.

Use Strong and Unique Passwords

Use long, unpredictable passwords and avoid using your name, birthday, or common words. Use a different password for every account so that one compromised password cannot be used to access your other accounts. Consider using a trusted password manager to securely store your passwords, and never share passwords through email, text, or phone.

Watch Out for Phishing

Avoid clicking suspicious links or downloading attachments from unknown sources. Cybercriminals may use fake emails, text messages, websites, or phone calls to appear like legitimate financial institutions. Instead of following a link in a message, visit your financial institution's official website directly or use its official app.

Use Secure Websites and Networks

When accessing financial accounts online, make sure the website uses HTTPS. Avoid accessing financial accounts through public computers or unsecured public Wi-Fi networks, where your information could be intercepted. When possible, use your own trusted device and a secure network.

Monitor Your Financial Accounts

Review account activity, statements, and security notifications regularly. Look for unfamiliar transactions, missing funds, unexpected account changes, or other unusual activity. Contact your financial institution immediately if you notice something suspicious.

Enable Multi-Factor Authentication

Enable multi-factor authentication (MFA) whenever it is available. MFA adds another layer of protection by requiring more than one type of verification, such as a password combined with a security code or biometric verification.
